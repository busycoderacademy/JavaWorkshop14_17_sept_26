Muhammad Ali was one of the greatest boxers in the history of the sport and a larger-than-life cultural figure. Born Cassius Marcellus Clay Jr. in 1942, he won Olympic gold in 1960 and became the youngest heavyweight champion of his era. Famous for his extraordinary speed, footwork, confidence, and sharp wit, Ali transformed boxing from a sport into a global spectacle.

Beyond the ring, Ali was known for his outspoken personality and willingness to stand by his convictions, even when doing so came at a significant personal cost. His famous “float like a butterfly, sting like a bee” style captured both his athletic brilliance and his showmanship.
